# Variant Annotation with Functional Genomic Effects

## Objective

After variant calling and VCF normalisation, the identified variants were annotated to determine their potential functional effects on known genomic features.

This step helps answer important questions such as:

- Which gene is affected by a variant?
- Does the variant occur in a coding or non-coding region?
- Does the variant cause an amino acid change?
- What is the predicted impact of the variant?

---

## Tool Used

**SnpEff eff**

SnpEff is a variant annotation tool that predicts the effects of genetic variants on known genomic features, including:

- Genes
- Transcripts
- Coding regions
- Proteins

SnpEff annotations were required before generating the GEMINI database because GEMINI can parse SnpEff-annotated VCF files.

---

## Functional Annotation

SnpEff adds information describing the predicted functional effects of variants.

For example, variants may be associated with effects such as:

- Synonymous variants
- Missense variants
- Stop-gained variants
- Frameshift variants
- Intronic variants
- Splice-region variants

These annotations help prioritise variants according to their potential biological significance.

---

## Output

Running SnpEff produced two datasets:

### 1. Annotated VCF

The main output was a VCF file containing the original variant information together with predicted functional annotations.

The annotations were added to the **INFO column** of the VCF.

### 2. Summary Statistics Report

SnpEff also produced an HTML Summary Stats report.

This report contains general information about the annotated variants, including the distribution of variants across different genomic features.

---

## Why Is Functional Annotation Important?

Not all detected variants are equally likely to affect a biological phenotype.

For example:

- Some variants may be silent and have no effect on the protein.
- Some variants may cause an amino acid substitution.
- Some variants may introduce a premature stop codon.
- Some variants may cause a frameshift and significantly disrupt protein function.

Functional annotation therefore helps prioritise variants that may be more relevant to the disease phenotype.
